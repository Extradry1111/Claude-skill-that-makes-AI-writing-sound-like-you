# Modes: what humans actually do in each format

Read only the section you need.

## Email (work)
- One-line greeting ("Hi Sarah," or just "Sarah,"). No "hope this finds you well".
- The point or the ask goes in the first two sentences. One ask per email, with a deadline if there is one.
- 50–150 words for most emails. Short paragraphs of 1–3 sentences.
- Sign-off: their name, or "Thanks," plus their name. Never "Best regards" plus a closing pleasantry stacked on top.
- Subject line: say what it's about and what you need ("Q3 budget: need a yes by Fri"), not "Following Up".

## Slack / Teams / DM / text
- No greeting, no sign-off. Lowercase is fine if their voiceprint has it.
- One to three short lines. Split long messages into a thread or a doc link.
- No bullets for fewer than three items, and no bold headers.
- Questions go last so they're the thing people answer.

## LinkedIn post
- First line is the hook, and it should be a real fact or a real take, not "I'm thrilled to announce" or "Let that sink in".
- Normal paragraphs (2–4 sentences). Avoid the one-line staircase.
- At most one emoji, and never as bullets. No "Thoughts? 👇", "Agree?" or hashtag walls (0–3 hashtags, at the end).
- Specific beats inspirational. One story with a number in it outperforms five lessons.
- Gratitude: name people and what they did, instead of "my incredible team".

## Cover letter
- Open with why *this* company or role, in a sentence that couldn't be pasted into another letter.
- No "I am writing to express my interest", "passionate about" or "fast-paced environment".
- Two or three proof points, each a concrete thing they did, with a number when they have one. Don't invent numbers. Use a placeholder.
- 200–300 words. End with a plain line: "I'd love to talk about [specific thing]."
- Stays professional, so contractions are fine but no slang.

## Essay / blog post
- Have a take, and put it in the first paragraph.
- Vary paragraph length. Use subheadings only above ~800 words.
- No closing summary that restates the intro. End on the strongest specific point, or on an open question you actually care about.
- Examples and anecdotes beat abstractions every time.

## Tweet / X / Threads
- One idea. No "🧵 1/" unless it truly needs a thread.
- Lowercase and fragments are culturally normal here, as long as the voiceprint fits.
- No hashtags unless it's an event tag.

## Docs / README / technical writing
- Clarity beats personality. Formal is fine, padding isn't.
- Bold-label bullets and headers are *appropriate* here, so don't strip structure.
- Cut "seamless", "robust", "powerful", "blazing fast" and "simply". Show a command or a benchmark instead.
- Second person ("you run...") and the imperative ("Run...") read best.

## Bio / About me
- Third person for conference bios, first person for personal sites. Ask if it's unclear.
- Two or three concrete facts (what they do, one notable thing, one human detail). No "passionate", "driven" or "visionary".

## Academic / formal report
- Aim for clear, not casual. Keep the hedges that are epistemically real ("the data suggest") and cut the decorative ones.
- Keep technical terms. Cut "delve", "tapestry", "in the realm of", "it is worth noting" and "plays a crucial role".
- Score target ≤ 25, not ≤ 15. Formal prose legitimately scores a bit higher.
