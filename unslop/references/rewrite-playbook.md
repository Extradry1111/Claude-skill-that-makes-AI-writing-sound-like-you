# Rewrite playbook

## The order of operations

Work through these in order. Earlier steps make later ones easier, and most texts are done by step 4.

1. **Find the point.** In one sentence, what does this text need the reader to know or do? Write it down for yourself. If the original has no point, that's the real problem. Ask the user, or make the point the first line.
2. **Delete.** Openers, closers, hedges, intensifiers, drumrolls, the summary paragraph, any sentence that only says something is important. A good unslop is often 30–50% shorter. Shorter isn't the goal, though. Never cut a fact or an ask.
3. **Flatten the fake contrasts.** "It's not X, it's Y" becomes "Y". "The result? Z." becomes "Z."
4. **Swap the inflated words** for plain ones, or better, for a detail from the source.
5. **Fix the rhythm.** Read the sentence lengths. If they're all similar, merge two, split one, and cut one down to four words.
6. **Apply the voiceprint.** Casing, contractions, punctuation habits, sentence length, greeting and sign-off.
7. **Read it as the recipient.** Would this person, getting this from this sender, think it came from a person? Fix whatever snags.

## Worked example: email

**Before (score 96)**

> Hi Sarah,
>
> I hope this email finds you well! I wanted to follow up on our conversation from last Tuesday regarding the Q3 marketing budget.
>
> It's important to note that the proposed $45,000 allocation plays a crucial role in our ability to navigate the complexities of the upcoming product launch on October 14. Moreover, this investment will help to enhance our brand visibility, foster deeper customer engagement, and drive meaningful results.
>
> Ultimately, I truly believe this represents a pivotal opportunity for our team.
>
> Please don't hesitate to reach out if you have any questions. I hope this helps!

**After (score ~10)**

> Hi Sarah,
>
> Quick follow-up on Tuesday's chat about the Q3 marketing budget.
>
> I'm asking for $45,000. Most of it goes to the launch on October 14, mainly [what it's for: paid search? events?]. If we cut it, [what happens?].
>
> Can you give me a yes or no by [date]?

What happened:
- The point ("approve $45k") moved to the front.
- "brand visibility, customer engagement, meaningful results" was a triplet of nothing. It became a placeholder for what the money *actually* buys, because only the sender knows that.
- An ask with a deadline replaced "don't hesitate to reach out". A real email needs a next step.
- $45,000, October 14, Q3 and Tuesday all survived (Fact Lock).

## Worked example: LinkedIn

**Before (score 98)**

> I'm thrilled to announce that I've joined Northwind as Head of Growth! 🚀
>
> This isn't just a new role — it's a new chapter in my journey.
>
> The secret? It's simple: listen to your customers.
>
> Let that sink in.
>
> Thoughts? 👇

**After (score ~3)**

> New job: I'm Head of Growth at Northwind, starting Monday.
>
> Six years at Contoso taught me one boring thing. Read every support ticket, every week, and build what keeps showing up. That took us from 40,000 users to 1.2M.
>
> If you do growth at a B2B company and want to compare notes, my DMs are open.

What happened: the news went first, the drumroll became one specific habit, the numbers stayed, and the "Thoughts?" bait became a real invitation.

## Things that are NOT slop (don't "fix" them)

- A single em-dash, a single triplet, or a single "crucial" in a 600-word piece. Density is what gives text away.
- Technical vocabulary that is just correct ("robust" in a statistics paper, "leverage" in finance).
- Formal register where formal is right (legal, academic, a letter to a government office). Unslop it toward *clear*, not toward *casual*.
- The user's own pet phrases. If their voiceprint uses "honestly" a lot, keep it.

## Overcorrection: the second uncanny valley

The fix for "sounds like ChatGPT" is not "sounds like ChatGPT pretending to be a teenager". Avoid:
- inserted typos or "um" and "lol" that the person doesn't use
- every sentence starting with "So" or "Look,"
- swapping every em-dash for a colon or semicolon (a new tic)
- choppy sentence fragments. Everywhere. For effect. (That's its own tell.)
- slang that dates the text or doesn't match the sender's age or job
